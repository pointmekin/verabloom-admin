# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Primary: Chompooh, Meen, and Kan, the shop's three owners. They share one admin login and use
it on their phones between making flowers and answering customers on LINE, Instagram, and
TikTok. A typical admin session is short: open a pending request, confirm a price, record a
payment, note who is making the flowers, and get back to work.

Secondary: Thai customers browsing the public catalog on a phone. They look through visible
products, pick a variation, and send one order request with their contact details. They do not
create an account and do not return to the app after that; follow-up happens on their social
channel of choice.

## Product Purpose

Verabloom replaces scattered chat threads with one shared system for the shop's actual
workflow: catalog, order requests, orders, payments, and expenses. Customers browse a mobile
catalog and submit a request; they get a `VB-` reference to screenshot, not an account. Admins
turn requests into confirmed orders, record partial or full payments, track which team member
is making each order's flowers, log shop expenses, and see received income, expenses, and net
cash for a period.

The system stays deliberately small. An order has one product variation and a quantity. The
final price is agreed outside the system, on the customer's channel of choice, and only
recorded here when the shop confirms the order. Success looks like: the three owners never lose
a request, always know what is owed, and never have to leave their phone to check.

## Brand Personality

Playful, warm, handmade. Verabloom's bouquets are hand-crafted flower arrangements with doggy
faces worked into the design, so the brand is affectionate and a little cheeky, never a formal
luxury florist. On the customer side, that charm should show in the catalog and the wordmark.
On the admin side, personality steps back: the three owners need speed and clarity while
standing at a workbench, not a delightful surprise mid-task.

## Anti-references

- A full ecommerce storefront: carts, checkout flows, saved payment methods, account chrome.
  Verabloom has one product per order and no customer accounts by design.
- Accounting software: dense ledgers, audit trails, reconciliation views. Verabloom tracks
  income, expenses, and net cash simply, not as a bookkeeping system.
- Cold, minimal luxury-florist sites that strip out warmth and playfulness for white space and
  restraint. That reads as a different, more expensive brand than Verabloom is.
- The generic SaaS admin dashboard: hero-metric tiles, gradient KPI cards, an eyebrow above
  every section. None of that fits a three-person flower shop's phone workflow.

## Design Principles

1. The three owners come first. When product and admin needs conflict, admin speed wins.
2. One hand, one phone. Every admin task must be completable one-handed on a phone screen.
3. The dogs carry the charm. Personality and delight belong to the catalog, not to admin chrome.
4. Snapshots are history. No UI convenience may rewrite what a past order actually recorded.
5. Thai first, always. English is the alternate translation, never the source of truth.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Keep it pragmatic, not a formal program: readable text contrast (avoid light gray body text),
a `prefers-reduced-motion` alternative for any animation, comfortable touch targets for one-handed
phone use, and Thai and English strings that both fit without clipping or layout breakage. No
WCAG compliance audit is in scope for this project.
